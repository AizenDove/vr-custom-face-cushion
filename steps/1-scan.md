# 1. Scan your face

Use [Polycam](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ai.polycam) on your phone to make a 3D model of your face.

<!-- PHOTO / VIDEO: scanning setup -->

## The scan doesn't need to be perfect

A low-poly mesh without colour works fine. Your face won't feel a 1 mm difference, so don't spend time on a high-detail scan. What matters is the overall shape of the forehead, cheekbones, temples and the bridge of the nose.

## Tips for a good scan

- **Even light.** Stand facing a window or a soft lamp. Avoid hard shadows and backlight.
- **Keep still.** Have someone else hold the phone and walk slowly around you, or sit on a swivel chair and turn slowly.
- **Neutral face.** Relax your face the way it is when you wear the headset. Don't smile or squint.
- **Hair away.** Tie your hair back and clip it away from the forehead and temples, where the gasket sits.
- **Cover the whole area.** Make sure the forehead, cheekbones, temples and the bridge of the nose are all captured.

## Take reference photos too

While you're at it, take a few photos of yourself **wearing the headset**, from the front and from the side. They show how the headset actually sits on your face, and you'll use them to place the gasket in Blender.

## Export

1. Open the finished scan in Polycam.
2. Export it as **OBJ** or **STL**.
3. Send the file to your computer.

> Check which export formats your Polycam plan allows. If your format is locked, try another of the free formats Blender can import (OBJ, STL, GLB/glTF).
